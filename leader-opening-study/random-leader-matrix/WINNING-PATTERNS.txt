Winning patterns in random leader self-play
============================================
Scope
-----
Exploratory analysis of 514 decisive games from the 600-game matrix. Excludes 72 tied and 14 unfinished games.
Each winner/loser comparison is within the same game, hence the same startup purchase pool, shared Worship events, and completed-turn count.
We also checked 24 fixed (leader, opposing leader, seat) groups, comparing that leader's wins with its losses.
This is descriptive outcome-conditioned analysis; no random policy was modified and no causal card-strength or significance claim is made.
winning-patterns.ts replays all 514 games and verifies final scores before recording early income, cash-floor exposure and source-attributed trashing.

Retained income is the strongest common pattern
-----------------------------------------------
Per decisive game, mean winner / loser:
- Final Talent count: 4.08 / 2.37.
- Final Drachma count: 11.10 / 7.26.
- Total Treasure face-value income trashed: $8.67 / $15.07.
- Final deck size: 34.02 / 21.36.
- Coins generated in first ten turns: $42.46 / $38.29.
- Fraction of own turns starting with whole-deck income capacity <= $4: 2.53% / 11.07%.
The coin-trash number is face value of removed Treasures, not purchase cost or cumulative lost earnings.
Winners have more Talents in 20/24 fixed leader/opponent/seat comparisons, more Drachmas in 21/24, and trash less Treasure income in 20/24.
All four leaders separately have lower average Treasure income trashed in their wins than losses:
Doreios $18.99 / $21.56; Thaleia $9.74 / $16.69; Nereon $5.70 / $8.45; Melia $6.08 / $8.65.
Random trashing still destroys useful economy even though the floor prevents dropping below $3.
This does not imply that deliberate thinning is weak.

Action purchases, conditioning on that card being in the shared startup pool
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mean purchased copies by winner / loser, with number of decisive games:
Merchant Fleet: 4.33 / 2.63 (52 games).
Sacred Grove: 3.72 / 2.59 (101 games).
Council of Sages: 4.09 / 3.35 (86 games).
Harbor Pilot: 3.59 / 3.40 (101 games).
Seed Keeper: 3.60 / 3.95 (43 games).
Both players could purchase the card in every included game. Effect gains may obtain cards outside the purchase pool and are not counted as purchases.
Merchant Fleet and Sacred Grove are the clearest purchase associations among these examples, consistent with extra income/Buys and extra gains. Greater early income can itself make these purchases possible; this is not proof that buying more causes the win.
Larger winning decks inflate raw final copy counts. Per ten final-deck cards, Harbor Pilot is actually less frequent in winners (0.426 vs 0.488), while Talent remains higher (1.200 vs 0.964).
No single Action appears as a universal winning engine.

Worship, only in games where the event is shared and available
-------------------------------------------------------------
Mean uses per winner / loser:
Tribute of the Tides: 7.38 / 7.01 (268 games).
Counsel of Olympus: 7.67 / 8.46 (261 games).
Blessing of the Fields: 7.68 / 8.49 (251 games).
Trial of the Spear: 6.39 / 6.50 (248 games).
The directions are the same after dividing each player's uses by its completed turns.
Winners use Worship less frequently per turn in 19/24 fixed leader/opponent/seat comparisons.
There is no broad association of repeated Worship with winning. Tribute shows a small positive association; the other three show small negative associations. These differences do not establish causal benefits or costs.
Worship happens after playing Treasures in this policy. Thus the immediate random cash-destruction mechanism is principally Seed Keeper/Forge and Doreios's leader trigger, not trashing Treasure through Worship.
Seed Keeper removes $5.22 of Treasure income per winning player versus $8.30 per losing player, across the decisive-game sample.

Interpretation
--------------
Winning random bots tend to preserve/build a larger working economy, escape the $3 floor, and acquire Talents, Merchant Fleets and Sacred Groves more often.
The current experiment is heavily influenced by random destruction of useful cards. It does not yet isolate leader power strength or an overpowered card/Worship combination.
The shared pool removes availability asymmetry inside each game, but early luck, leader powers, purchase affordability, deck size and survival all remain intertwined.
The matched startup pairs and reused seed banks also mean these observations are not independent hypothesis tests.

# Four new divine houses: revised rules proposal

Status: **implemented for human playtesting; not balance-tested**. Play at `/expansion/`; review individual effects at `/dev/animations/`. The published gallery/print catalog and trained bots remain on the original card set.

This revision uses a twelve-pile Action market, replaces public-memory Echo with borrowed cards, makes each Deploy grant repeat one Action or Treasure, and replaces immobilization markers with topdecking opponents’ Territories. Card costs and quantities below are proposed starting values.

## 1. Setup and rules shared by all houses

### Twelve Action piles in every game

Each divine house supplies one leader, one starting Temple, three regular Action cards, and one worship event.

After the leader draft, include all three Action piles belonging to each chosen leader’s house. If fewer than four houses were drafted, randomly choose enough distinct undrafted houses to bring the market to four houses. Include all three Action piles from each of those houses.

Thus a two-player game has two drafted houses and two random houses; a three-player game has three drafted houses and one random house; a four-player game uses exactly its four drafted houses. The market always contains **twelve regular Action piles**, plus the existing six Treasure/Territory supply piles. Random selection happens once during setup and is public and fixed for that game.

Only drafted leaders’ worship events enter play. A randomly added house contributes its three Action piles, not its leader, Temple, or worship event. Everyone may buy any available Action or use any available worship event, regardless of affiliation.

This replaces the earlier assumption that all houses’ Action piles are always present. It applies to the original four houses too. Consequently, a two-player game cannot assume every original Action or every worship event is available.

### Players, cards, and supply

Keep two to four players, the reverse-order leader draft, and equal turns. Draft from all eight unique leaders. Starting decks remain six Obols, three Hamlets, and the chosen leader’s unique Temple.

Regular Action piles retain 8/12/16 copies for two/three/four players. Basic supply quantities remain unchanged. Temples have no supply piles and cannot be bought or gained. Each Temple has printed cost 0, is an Action of its house, and grants +1 Worship and +1 Action.

The existing Acropolis-empty or three-empty-supply-piles ending still applies. Finish the round so everyone has equal turns. Empty personal decks and discards do not count as empty supply piles.

### Treasure plays during buying

**Proposed simple rule: during the Treasure or Buy phase, you may play a Treasure from your hand between completed effects and purchases, even after buying a card.** Its Coins are available for subsequent purchases or worship that turn. Playing it costs neither an Action nor a Buy.

This includes Treasures drawn, found by Seek, or otherwise put into hand during buying. To avoid tracking when each card entered the hand, it also includes Treasures deliberately retained earlier. This is an explicit general relaxation of the first-purchase restriction, not a Theseus-only exception.

An effect must finish before you voluntarily play a Treasure. You cannot interrupt Seek, an Echo resolution, or a repeated Action to spend cards just found. Treasures gained to discard or deck are still unavailable until another effect puts them in hand. Buying still requires an available Buy. None of this reopens ordinary Action play.

### Worship, Devotion, and leader triggers

Worship costs one Worship resource plus the event’s Coin cost and uses no Buy. Check Devotion when paying: two matching Actions in your play area enable Favored instead of Standard. Resources generated by the event cannot pay its own upfront cost.

A leader triggers once per own turn on the first matching Action **actually played by that player**, including a borrowed Action played through Echo. Claim the trigger when the Action enters play, before resolving its effects, so nested plays cannot claim it again. Resolve the leader ability after that Action finishes its entire resolution, including any Deploy repetitions and nested Echo effects.

Repeating one Action’s effect is still one actual card play. It contributes one Devotion and at most one leader trigger. Using a worship event itself does not trigger a leader.

### Proposed patrons

Echo is affiliated with Zeus. Hermes for Theseus, Nike for Achilles, and Hades for Perseus remain provisional game affiliations for review. The following mechanics do not depend on keeping those patron names.

## 2. Echo / Zeus — borrow from an opponent’s discard

### The Echo icon and its number

Echo gets a distinct operation icon: **a solid central sound mark with two outward repeating arcs**, readable in monochrome and distinguishable from the existing lightning-bolt Action icon. This is an icon design specification; artwork is still to be made.

Print **+N followed by the Echo icon** using the same quantity treatment as other positive effects. In this document, **+1 Echo** and **+2 Echo** stand for that notation. The number counts separate Echo opportunities resolved now, in order. It is not a Coin limit, a persistent resource, or the number of times to repeat the same borrowed card.

Each opportunity is optional. Finish one, including any nested effects, before choosing the next. Unused opportunities expire when that instruction finishes.

### Resolve one Echo opportunity

Choose an opponent. Inspect their current discard pile, which is public.

If their discard is nonempty, choose one Action from it and play that card’s effect for yourself without spending an Action. Any Action is eligible, including a Temple or a card with Echo, Seek, Deploy, or Bind. If the pile contains no Action, this opponent offers no playable card; you may choose another opponent or decline the opportunity. **A nonempty discard containing only Treasures or Territories does not enable the empty-discard fallback.**

If the chosen opponent’s discard is empty, discard the top card of their deck face up. If it is an Action, borrow and play it. If it is a Treasure, borrow and play it for its Treasure effect. If it is a Territory, leave it in their discard; it has no play effect. That opportunity is spent regardless of what is revealed. If both their deck and discard are empty, nothing happens; do not shuffle or invent a card.

For the fallback, this draft interprets “play its effect if any” to include a Treasure’s Coin effect. Direct selection from a nonempty discard remains Action-only.

### Ownership and return

Move a borrowed Action or Treasure into a clearly marked borrowed section of **your play area**, with its owner identified. It stays there until your cleanup, then returns to its owner’s discard pile. It is never gained or transferred into your deck. A borrowed card still belongs to its original owner for scoring and inventory purposes.

The fallback card visibly enters its owner’s discard before being borrowed. A Territory stays there. A borrowed Action or Treasure leaves that discard and cannot be selected there again while it is being played or held in your play area.

A borrowed Action contributes its printed god’s Devotion to you while in play. It can trigger your leader, never its owner’s leader. A borrowed Treasure produces its printed resources for you. Neither gets a second physical copy. At cleanup, discard your own cards to your discard and return borrowed cards to their respective owners before drawing your next hand.

Holding borrowed cards until cleanup makes recursion finite without a blacklist: an Echo card cannot immediately select itself from a discard, and a nested chain must find another physical card. Each borrowed card is outside its owner’s deck and discard until returned. If several borrowed cards return to one owner, place them in their discard in their original borrowing order; discard contents remain fully inspectable.

### Resolving the borrowed effect

Read its instructions as addressed to the borrowing player. Use your hand, deck, resources, choices, and the current supply; pay its costs anew. An opponent’s Forge of Heroes requires you to trash your own card before gaining. An opponent’s Seek searches your deck. An opponent’s Bind can target any of your opponents, including the borrowed card’s owner.

A borrowed Echo card resolves new Echo opportunities normally. A borrowed Deploy card prepares your next Action or Treasure, following the Deploy rules below. There is no public-memory record or list of earlier turns to maintain.

Echo can play its borrowed card during any phase in which the source effect resolves. This is explicit permission for that particular play; it does not generally reopen the Actions phase. A fallback Treasure can therefore produce Coins even during Actions. Ordinary Treasures in hand still wait until Treasures or Buys.

### Echo, Voice of the Thunder

**Leader · Zeus.**

After you resolve the first Zeus Action you play this turn: **+1 Echo.**

The leader now grants the same Echo operation as the cards. There is no separate resource-borrowing rule. If her first Zeus Action borrows another Zeus Action, the leader trigger is already claimed by the outer card and occurs once after the outer effect finishes.

### Temple of Zeus

**Unique starting Action · Zeus · cost 0.**

**+1 Worship; +1 Action.**

### Borrowed Voice

**Action · Zeus · cost 2.**

**+1 Echo.**

It has no automatic replacement Action. Whether it extends the turn depends on the borrowed card and any leader trigger.

### Thunder’s Answer

**Action · Zeus · cost 4.**

**+1 Card; +1 Echo.**

Draw first, then choose the opponent and borrowed effect with knowledge of your updated hand.

### Chorus of Olympus

**Action · Zeus · cost 5.**

**+1 Action; +2 Echo.**

Choose each opportunity separately. You can target the same opponent again, but a card already borrowed is unavailable. If the first opportunity emptied their discard, the second can use the deck fallback.

### Reverberation

**Worship event · Zeus · cost 3 Coins plus 1 Worship.**

**Standard: +1 Echo.**

### Example and implications

Echo plays her Temple and triggers +1 Echo. An opponent has Sacred Academy in their discard. She borrows it, draws two cards, and gains one Action. It remains in her play area, contributes one Athena Devotion, and returns to its owner’s discard at her cleanup.

Alternatively, an opponent has an empty discard. Echo discards their top card: a Drachma. She borrows it and receives two Coins even though she is still in Actions. A Hamlet would instead remain in the opponent’s discard and give no benefit.

Echo can interact from the opening round without recorded history. Her strength depends on current discard contents and opponents’ shuffle timing. Deck fallback also disrupts opponents’ future draws and reveals information; this is an interaction effect as well as an engine benefit.

## 3. Theseus / Hermes — navigate the deck

### Seek N for a stated card type

Reveal the top N cards of your deck, or as many as available. After the reveal finishes, choose up to one revealed card of the stated type, put it into your hand, and discard the rest. “Any card” includes Actions, Treasures, and Territories. You may keep none, but cannot stop revealing early by choice.

Revealed cards wait face up outside the deck, hand, discard, and play area until the whole Seek finishes. If the deck empties, shuffle the existing discard and continue. Do not include cards currently held by this Seek in that shuffle. If both available piles run out, stop and choose from those revealed.

Finding a card is not gaining or playing it. A found Action needs normal permission to be played, such as an available Action in the Actions phase or a pending Deploy. A found Treasure can be played after Seek finishes during Treasures or Buys, including after an earlier purchase.

### Theseus, Walker of the Labyrinth

**Leader · Hermes.**

After you resolve the first Hermes Action you play this turn, **Seek 2 for an Action.**

### Temple of Hermes

**Unique starting Action · Hermes · cost 0.**

**+1 Worship; +1 Action.**

### Ariadne’s Thread

**Action · Hermes · cost 2.**

**+1 Action. Seek 3 for a Treasure.**

### Labyrinth Scout

**Action · Hermes · cost 4.**

**+1 Action. Seek 4 for an Action.**

### Wayfinder

**Action · Hermes · cost 5.**

**+1 Card; +1 Action. Seek 3 for any card.**

### Open the Hidden Way

**Worship event · Hermes · cost 3 Coins plus 1 Worship.**

**Standard: Seek 4 for any card.**

**Favored: Seek 6 for any card; +1 Action.**

An Action gained from the resource bonus does not reopen Actions during buying. A pending Deploy can still permit the next Action card to be played then. If the card found is a Treasure, the revised Treasure rule permits playing it after this event finishes.

### Example and implications

During Buys, Theseus has already bought a card but has another Buy and enough Coins to worship. He pays for Open the Hidden Way, finds a Talent, puts it into hand, and discards the other revealed cards. Once the whole event finishes, he can play the Talent for three Coins and make another purchase.

Seeking gives selection and deck cycling but may discard useful cards along the way. It can also clear Territories Perseus put on top, trading the search effect’s capacity against that interference.

## 4. Achilles / Nike — repeat the next Action

### Deploy N: one Action, Treasure or Territory, N complete resolutions

**Deploy N prepares the next single Action, Treasure or Territory you play this turn: play it without spending an Action and resolve its effect N times in total.** A deployed Territory draws one card per activation: Deploy 3 draws three cards. It remains one physical Territory in play, keeps its scoring value, grants no Devotion, and does not trigger an Action-only leader ability. Like a deployed Treasure, it preserves the current phase. It cannot be played without a Deploy grant. Draws and shuffles follow the ordinary rules and block undo when they reveal information. Deploy 1 means one free play; Deploy 2 means twice; Deploy 3 means three times. N is the total, not the number of extra repetitions.

The Deploy instruction itself does not select or play a card. Finish the current effect and leader trigger, then choose the next Action, Treasure or Territory from your hand when you are ready. That card can have been drawn by the Deploy source. You may decline to play another card; pending Deploy expires at cleanup.

Deploy can permit that next Action, Treasure or Territory during Actions, Treasures, or Buys. It does not restore general Action-phase permissions. If you are in Buys and the repeated card gives Actions, those counters alone cannot play further Actions; another queued Deploy grant can.

The next Action, Treasure or Territory actually played consumes the oldest pending Deploy grant automatically, including an Action borrowed by Echo during a nested effect. A Treasure borrowed through Echo also consumes the oldest grant. Worship does not consume a grant.

### Repeat effects, not physical cards

Put the selected card into play once. Resolve its complete text in order, then repeat from the beginning until all N resolutions finish. Each resolution draws its own cards, pays its own costs, checks its own conditions, and makes fresh choices. Nothing is cloned; an Action contributes one Devotion, not N. A Treasure contributes no Devotion.

No voluntary action, Treasure play, purchase, or worship can interrupt those repetitions. Required choices and nested Echo effects finish in their normal order. Resolve the source card’s claimed leader ability once after the entire repeated effect finishes.

If the repeated card grants Deploy, those new grants prepare the **next different Action, Treasure or Territory play**. They do not increase the repetitions already assigned to the card currently resolving. A nested Echo that plays another Action can consume a newly granted Deploy before the outer card finishes; its remaining repetitions then continue normally.

### Several pending grants

Each Deploy grant stays independent in a first-in, first-out queue. The next Action, Treasure or Territory consumes only the oldest grant and resolves that many times without spending an Action. The remaining grants apply to later Action, Treasure or Territory plays; their counts are never combined.

For example, Deploy 2 followed by Deploy 3 permits one Action to resolve twice, then another Action to resolve three times. Two Deploy 1 grants permit two successive free Action, Treasure or Territory plays. If a Deploy 2 repeats a card that grants Deploy 2, its two resolutions append two separate Deploy 2 grants behind any grants already waiting.

New grants do not change the repetitions of the card currently resolving. All unused grants expire at cleanup.

A deployed Treasure produces its printed coins N times, stays one physical card, and does not spend an Action or advance the phase. For example, Deploy 3 on Talent produces 9 Coins while leaving Actions open. It remains a Treasure: it does not trigger Action-only leader abilities or contribute Action Devotion. An undeployed Treasure follows the normal phase rules.

Play All Treasures processes cards in its existing order. Each Treasure consumes one available grant; once the queue is empty, remaining Treasures are played normally. You can play cards individually to choose which Treasure receives which grant.

### Achilles, Spearhead of Victory

**Leader · Nike.**

After you resolve the first Nike Action you play this turn, **Deploy 1.**

This appends an independent Deploy 1 grant. It permits an additional free Action, Treasure or Territory play after any earlier grants have been used.

**+1 Worship; +1 Action.**

### Rallying Cry

The former cost limit is removed so every Deploy grant has the same Action-or-Treasure eligibility; there is no queue of permissions that apply to different cost ranges.

### Myrmidon Vanguard

**Action · Nike · cost 4.**

**+2 Coins. Deploy 1.**

### Phalanx Advance

**Action · Nike · cost 5.**

**+1 Card. Deploy 2.**

This doubles one next Action, Treasure or Territory instead of allowing two different cards to be played. Favored March of Victory grants Deploy 3, resolving the next Action, Treasure or Territory three times.

### March of Victory

**Worship event · Nike · cost 3 Coins plus 1 Worship.**

**Standard: Deploy 1.**

**Favored: Deploy 3.**

During Buys, the permission can be used after the event finishes to play an Action from hand. Drawing Treasures with that Action can then fund further purchases or worship once its entire repeated effect and leader trigger finish.

### Example and implications

Achilles plays Phalanx Advance as his first Nike Action. It draws one card and gives Deploy 2. His leader then gives Deploy 1. These remain separate: the next Action resolves twice, and the following Action resolves once, both without spending Actions.

He chooses Harvest Feast. Without spending an Action, he draws two cards, gains one Action, and discards one card; then does the whole sequence again, with a fresh discard choice. Harvest Feast enters play once and contributes one Demeter Devotion. Achilles’s Nike leader does not trigger again. His queued Deploy 1 remains available for another Action, Treasure or Territory after Harvest Feast finishes.

Deploy rewards high-value effects and lets a small number of terminal cards do more work. It can produce large resource swings, repeated trash/gain decisions, or repeated attacks. It cannot pay a conditional effect’s cost only once and receive the reward repeatedly.

## 5. Perseus / Hades — return Territories to the top

### Bind N

**Bind N means: choose up to N Territory cards from opponents’ discard piles and put them on top of their owners’ decks.** The acting player chooses both the physical cards and their top-to-bottom order when several go to one owner. They may come from one opponent or several.

Choose the whole set and ordering, then move it as one effect. A selected physical card can be moved only once by that effect. All choices and moved identities are public. Existing cards in the deck retain their order underneath the added Territories. Do not inspect, reveal, or shuffle those existing cards. An empty deck can receive Territories normally.

Cards never change ownership. Bind cannot target hands, cards in play, the supply, already-topdecked cards, or your own discard. If no opponent has a Territory in discard, Bind does nothing. Nothing is discarded from a deck as a fallback.

There are no Bind markers, locked cards, delayed choices, protection states, or special cleanup rules. The interference is entirely represented by real cards sitting on top of real decks.

### Repeating Bind

Each separate Bind instruction examines the discard piles as they currently stand. Repeating a Bind effect can move additional Territories, but cannot select one already moved onto a deck. The available Territory cards are the natural limit; this draft adds no per-turn attack cap.

If a later effect puts a previously moved Territory back in its owner’s discard, a later Bind may choose it again. For repeated Bind instructions affecting the same owner, the later instruction’s cards go above those placed by the earlier instruction.

### Perseus, Bearer of the Gorgon

**Leader · Hades.**

After you resolve the first Hades Action you play this turn, **Bind 1.**

### Temple of Hades

**Unique starting Action · Hades · cost 0.**

**+1 Worship; +1 Action.**

### Polished Shield

**Action · Hades · cost 2.**

**+1 Card; +1 Action. You may discard 1 Territory from your hand. If you do, +1 Card.**

This replaces the obsolete locked-card rescue. It gives a player who drew a topdecked Territory a way to exchange it for another card. It is useful with ordinary starting Hamlets as well. The discard is optional and is not trashing: the Territory keeps its VP and can be targeted again while in discard.

### Gorgon’s Gaze

**Action · Hades · cost 4.**

**+2 Coins. Bind 1.**

### Harpe Bearer

**Action · Hades · cost 5.**

**+2 Cards. Bind 1.**

### Stillness of the Underworld

**Worship event · Hades · cost 3 Coins plus 1 Worship.**

**Standard: +1 Coin. Bind 1.**

**Favored: +2 Coins. Bind 2.**

Receive the Coin bonus after paying the full cost. The net Coin cost is two for Standard and one for Favored, with one Worship spent either way. No eligible Territory is required to receive the Coin bonus. The effect does not refund Worship.

### Example and implications

Perseus plays Gorgon’s Gaze as his first Hades Action. He gains two Coins and topdecks a Hamlet from Melia’s discard. After the card finishes, his leader Binds again; he topdecks a Polis from her discard above the Hamlet. Melia’s next two draws will be Polis then Hamlet unless another effect changes her deck first.

A player normally already holds the hand drawn at the end of their previous turn. Bind therefore often disrupts their next extra draws or the hand drawn at their next cleanup, **not the hand they already hold**. It never forcibly skips a phase or a turn.

Bind can be strong against drawing engines, but it can also help someone cycle around scoring cards, especially if they can Seek or discard them. When Bind empties an opponent’s discard, their next Echo fallback is predictably a Territory unless another effect changes the deck first. Near the end, Bind changes draw quality rather than anyone’s VP or ownership.

## 6. Interactions that must be explicit

### Echo and Deploy

A borrowed Action is a real play for the borrower and can consume pending Deploy. It stays one physical card and returns to its owner once, after all repetitions and at cleanup. Repeat costs are paid with the borrower’s cards and resources each time.

If a repeated Zeus Action gives +1 Echo, each repetition resolves a new Echo opportunity. Cards already borrowed are unavailable in their owners’ discards. If an echoed card grants Deploy, it prepares the borrower’s next actual Action, Treasure or Territory, which may be found by a subsequent Echo opportunity.

An Echo source with pending Deploy generated inside its own resolution is not its own next Action. Nested borrowed cards can consume those grants; otherwise they remain for a later Action, Treasure or Territory from hand. This prevents self-repetition loops while allowing deliberate combinations.

### Echo and Perseus

An empty-discard fallback can reveal a Territory and leave it in the opponent’s discard. A later Bind can topdeck that same card. A later Echo fallback might discard it again. Every such operation consumes its own instruction; there is no automatic repeated loop.

Bind can remove the last Territory from an opponent’s discard, making that pile empty and enabling Echo’s fallback. Conversely, a nonempty discard with only Territories blocks direct Action borrowing and does not permit the fallback.

A borrowed Hades card contributes Hades Devotion to the borrower and can enable Favored Hades worship if that event is in the game. It does not trigger Perseus unless Perseus is the player borrowing it.

### Seek, Bind, and shuffling

Seek reveals what is actually on top, including Territories placed there by Bind. It may discard those Territories while finding another card. It must complete its reveal and choice before an ordinary Treasure play or worship can occur.

Borrowed cards remain out of their owners’ deck and discard while being borrowed, so any shuffle during that time excludes them. The same is true of cards still being revealed by Seek. Return or discard them only at the specified time; do not silently include them in a shuffle to preserve an expected deck count.

### Leader triggers and repeated effects

Claim the first matching Action’s leader trigger at entry into play. A nested Action of the same god cannot take that claim. Resolve the claimed trigger after the claiming card’s complete repeated effect finishes.

For example, Echo repeats a Zeus card twice through Deploy. If it was her first Zeus Action, it produces its printed effects twice, then her leader grants +1 Echo once. A borrowed Temple of another god may instead trigger that god’s leader if that leader is the borrower and has not yet triggered.

### Buying, found Treasures, and outstanding permission

Playing a Treasure during Buys does not restore spent Buys or reopen Actions. Deploy is the explicit exception permitting the next Action. Echo is the explicit exception permitting the particular card it borrows. Finish each source effect, its repetitions, and its leader trigger before taking another voluntary step.

All unspent Actions, Coins, Buys, Worship, and pending Deploy expire at cleanup. Echo opportunities resolve within their source instruction and cannot be saved for later.

### Information and undo

Seek, drawing, and Echo’s deck fallback reveal new information and make undo unavailable across that reveal, even when the revealed card is an unusable Territory. Returning an already-public borrowed card or moving a public Territory from discard onto a deck does not alone reveal a hidden card.

Selecting a known discard Action for Echo is reversible only while its resulting effects have revealed no new information and the existing undo rules still allow it. Repetitions do not bypass an information boundary. Rewinding an eligible composite action must restore all ownership, borrowed-card locations, pending Deploy, trigger claims, and topdeck order together.

### End of game and scoring

The end-of-round check remains after cleanup. Return borrowed cards before that check and before scoring. Count Territories by ownership, not by which player last moved them. Bind does not grant VP, remove VP, add turns, or extend the final round. No new effect targets a player after the game has finished.

## 7. Presentation and physical play

### Setup

Show the four selected houses and distinguish drafted houses from random fill-ins. Animate or list the random selection publicly. Show exactly twelve regular Action piles. Supplemental houses’ worship events must not appear as usable events.

### Echo

Use the +N Echo icon on leader/card/event text. Show eligible opponents’ discard piles; an empty discard offers a face-down deck fallback, clearly identified as unknown before selection. Never preview the hidden card.

Animate the chosen card from its owner’s discard into the borrower’s play area. Mark the original owner on the borrowed card without changing its affiliation. Observers see the same selection and movement. At cleanup, animate it back to its owner’s discard. For a fallback, show the actual discard/reveal before any borrow. Physical play uses the same borrowed row and a simple owner marker; no turn-history ledger is needed.

### Seek

Reveal cards in the center, select one into hand, and discard the rest. Update deck/discard counts as the moves occur. Buying controls should allow playing a Treasure found in hand after resolution finishes.

### Deploy

Display how many total resolutions the next Action, Treasure or Territory will receive. Once that card is chosen, show one physical card with a visible “1 of N” resolution indicator. Animate its effects for each resolution without creating duplicate cards or Devotion. Show remaining grants separately for subsequent Action, Treasure or Territory plays.

### Bind

Let the acting player choose Territories directly from opponents’ public discards, then order multiple cards for the same deck. Animate each Territory onto its owner’s deck and show the public topdeck order. There is no defender dialog and no immobilization badge or recovery timer.

## 8. Remaining design choices and balance review

### Patron names

Confirm Hermes/Nike/Hades or choose other patron affiliations for Theseus/Achilles/Perseus.

### Echo strength and card handling

This draft allows any Action in an opponent’s discard, with no cost cap or forbidden keyword. It proposes holding borrowed cards until cleanup, allowing them to contribute Devotion and trigger the borrower’s leader, and allowing Treasures only through the empty-discard fallback. Those consequences should be approved together: they make Echo more than an abstract copy effect.

### Deploy stacking

Deploy N means N total resolutions of one Action, Treasure or Territory, including the free play. Multiple grants remain independent and are consumed in FIFO order; they never combine. The supply cards use Deploy 1 and Deploy 2; Favored March of Victory uses Deploy 3.

### Treasure timing

Confirm the simple general permission to play any Treasure in hand during Buys, including retained Treasures. It satisfies playing newly found Treasures without tagging their acquisition time.

### Repeated attacks and interaction

Bind now has no marker-based cap. Deploy can repeat it, Echo can borrow it, and several opponents can target the same player. The number of available Territories in discards limits immediate targeting, but draw/discard effects can expose them again. Measure how often a player draws a hand dominated by topdecked Territories and how often the attack helps rather than hurts.

The fixed-house market means counters are conditional on setup. Polished Shield is always available when Hades is present, because that house brings all three of its Actions; Hermes’s Seek cards are available only when Hermes is drafted or randomly included. Trash effects, +Buy sources, and draw engines may likewise be absent from a particular market.

### Starting costs and evaluation

The twelve Action costs and all leader/event numbers remain experimental. Echo can access an expensive discarded Action through a cheap source; Deploy can amplify strong terminal effects; Wayfinder can improve both draw quality and speed; Bind may concentrate interference on one opponent. Existing RL models do not understand these operations or variable markets and need new support and training before their results can establish balance.

First evaluate rules comprehension and effect timing, then compare win shares, ending turns, borrowed-card value, repeat counts, and attack impact across multiple drafted/random house combinations. This PR includes a playable engine and shared-screen review table, with no balance claim.

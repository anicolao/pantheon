# Two-purchase planner: frozen first comparison

Compare v19 two-purchase planning against v18 one-card acquisition. Keep the raw Money and Engine objectives, shared turn-2 ending, play controller, thinning and terminal sampler unchanged. Neutral base game: no leader powers or Worship, identical starting decks.

32 identical seed blocks per ordered cell: 16 new M/MT/E/ET matchups, eight new-v18 direct comparisons (four profiles, both seats), and four v18 mirrors. 896 games total. Use every available CPU. No aggregation across strategies or seats. Report direct win share (half a tied game), before/after against each fixed old opponent, and the new response matrix. This is a screening-sized comparison, not evidence of small differences or noninferiority. Keep all game records and one replay per cell. Audit every saved replay and validate every final inventory, score, seed and result.

Planner: every legal economic first purchase plus pass, all supply definitions plus no second purchase. A fixed continuation target buys at the earliest affordable legal opportunity now (if a Buy and cash remain), or over two future turns. Passing ends the current turn. Real game reducers handle payments, effects, gains/trashes, supply, cleanup and reshuffles. Eight common search shuffles select one continuation per first choice; eight disjoint investment validation shuffles compare those choices. Terminal decks use the unchanged stratified three-turn sampler. Score is two investment turns plus three terminal turns under exactly the v18 raw objective. Execute only the first purchase and replan.

Restrictions: economic Buy decisions only; scoring/endgame priorities and Action-effect gains are unchanged. No simulated opponent acquisitions, Worship, or adaptive fallback acquisition policy. Terminal fresh-shuffle estimate ignores the exact time to draw newly bought cards after the two-turn investment window. Two purchases cannot discover combinations requiring three or more. Terminal sample bank is the old fixed sampler, not an independent validation bank. Eight investment samples are deliberately bounded; selection noise remains possible. No objective tuning based on trial results.
